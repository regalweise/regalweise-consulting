Tokenized Deposits vs Stablecoins: Which Model Wins for Banks?

3 min read · September 2026 · RegalWeise Consulting

Banks have two routes to on-chain money: tokenize their own deposits, or participate in stablecoin ecosystems. They are often presented as rivals; in practice they solve different problems.

How They Differ

A tokenized deposit is a bank liability represented on a ledger, carrying deposit insurance treatment and the bank's credit risk. A stablecoin is issued by a separate entity against reserve assets.

  • Tokenized deposits stay on the balance sheet and keep the interest-bearing, regulated deposit relationship
  • Stablecoins offer wider public-network reach but sit outside the banking perimeter
  • Interoperability between the two is the unresolved design question

Where Each Fits

Tokenized deposits suit closed-loop and interbank use: intraday liquidity, wholesale settlement and corporate treasury. Stablecoins suit open ecosystems, such as payments to non-bank counterparties and digital marketplaces.

  • Wholesale and treasury flows: tokenized deposits
  • Open-network consumer and merchant payments: regulated stablecoins
  • Cross-border corridors: likely both, linked through shared standards

Our View

The strategic question is not which wins, but how a bank keeps customers' money inside its balance sheet while still reaching the networks where they want to spend it.
