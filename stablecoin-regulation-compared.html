Stablecoin Regulation Compared: GENIUS Act, MiCA, Hong Kong and India's Cautious Path

3 min read · October 2026 · RegalWeise Consulting

Stablecoin rules are arriving jurisdiction by jurisdiction, and they are not identical. For banks operating across borders, the differences in reserves, licensing and permitted issuers shape product design.

The Major Frameworks

The headline regimes share a common philosophy — fully backed, redeemable, supervised — but diverge in the detail. Check each regulator's current text before acting, as implementation rules continue to evolve.

  • United States: the GENIUS Act, signed in 2025, creates a federal framework for payment stablecoins with reserve and disclosure requirements
  • European Union: MiCA regulates e-money tokens and asset-referenced tokens, with authorisation and reserve rules
  • Hong Kong: a licensing regime for fiat-referenced stablecoin issuers administered by the HKMA
  • India: no dedicated stablecoin framework; the RBI has publicly voiced caution and favours its own CBDC

What This Means for Indian and APAC Banks

Even where a home regulator is cautious, banks with global clients face foreign stablecoin rules indirectly through correspondent relationships, treasury clients and group entities.

  • Map which stablecoins your clients and counterparties already touch
  • Build a jurisdiction matrix for permitted activities and reporting duties
  • Align AML, sanctions and liquidity policies before demand arrives

The Takeaway

Regulatory divergence is a design constraint, not a blocker. Banks that document it now will move faster when their own regulator acts.
