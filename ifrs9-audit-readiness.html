IFRS9 in Practice: Closing the Gap Between Model Design and Audit Readiness

3 min read · March 2025 · RegalWeise Consulting

Most banks that implemented IFRS 9 in 2018 did so under intense time pressure. The standard was complex, the data requirements unprecedented, and the modelling methodology largely untested in production. Seven years on, regulators and auditors are applying significantly more scrutiny — and many banks are finding gaps they need to close urgently.

The Core Challenge: Model Design vs. Operational Reality

IFRS 9 ECL models are typically designed by risk quants who understand the statistical methodology deeply. But these models must operate in a production environment managed by finance and IT teams who built systems to the specifications they were given at a point in time — often months before anyone fully understood what the model actually needed.

"An IFRS 9 model that produces the right number for the wrong reasons is not an IFRS 9 model that will survive regulatory scrutiny. Auditability isn't a feature — it's the foundation."

The Five Gaps We See Most Often

1. Staging Logic Inconsistency

Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR) criteria are often defined in model documentation with more precision than they are implemented in the staging engine. Edge cases — backstop triggers, cure period logic, forbearance flags — are frequently handled differently in production than the specification intended. Auditors test these scenarios specifically.

2. Macroeconomic Scenario Integration

IFRS 9 requires forward-looking, probability-weighted ECL estimates across multiple economic scenarios. Many banks implemented this with three fixed scenarios at go-live and have not revisited the scenario design or weighting methodology since — despite material changes in the economic outlook.

3. Data Lineage from Source to Model

Model validators and auditors increasingly require end-to-end data lineage — from source system transaction record, through data warehouse transformations, to the input dataset that feeds the model. Gaps in this lineage undermine the auditability of the ECL calculation.

4. Model Overlays Governance

Post-model management overlays proliferated rapidly during COVID-19 and have become permanent in many banks' IFRS 9 processes. The governance frameworks for approving, documenting, and unwinding overlays are often inadequate relative to their materiality.

5. Model Monitoring and Recalibration

IFRS 9 models were calibrated on pre-2018 data in most cases. The credit cycle since then has included a pandemic, a rapid rate-rising cycle, and significant sector-specific stresses. Model performance monitoring is often under-resourced relative to what regulators now expect.

Building Audit-Ready IFRS 9 Operations

  • Establish a model inventory linking every ECL model component to its production implementation, with version control and change management
  • Implement automated data lineage tracking from source systems to model inputs with reconciliation controls at each transformation step
  • Formalise the macroeconomic scenario review process with a documented governance framework including frequency, ownership, and escalation criteria
  • Create an overlay register with approval workflows, time limits, and unwinding triggers built into the governance framework
  • Deploy model monitoring dashboards that surface performance degradation signals to model owners on a monthly basis

The Regulatory Direction of Travel

Regulators including the PRA, ECB supervisory arm, and RBI are intensifying their focus on IFRS 9 implementation quality — not just the headline ECL number, but the integrity of the process that produces it. Banks that invest in closing these gaps now will face audits with confidence. Those that don't will face remediation programmes on a regulator's timeline.
