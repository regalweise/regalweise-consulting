Stablecoins Are Becoming Payment Infrastructure — Banks Need a Position

3 min read · October 2026 · RegalWeise Consulting

Stablecoins have moved from a crypto-trading tool to a serious settlement option for cross-border payments, treasury and e-commerce. Banks do not have to issue one, but every bank needs a clear stance on how it will interact with them.

Why the Conversation Has Changed

Regulation has given institutions something they lacked: a legal route to participate. Clear rules on reserves, redemption and licensing turn stablecoins from a risk topic into a product and infrastructure topic.

  • Faster, always-on settlement outside correspondent banking cut-off times
  • Programmable money that can be embedded in treasury and supply-chain workflows
  • Pressure on cross-border payment margins as corporates ask for alternatives

The Four Positions a Bank Can Take

Most banks will land on one of four postures, and the choice should be deliberate rather than reactive.

  • Observe: monitor regulation and client demand, with no exposure
  • Connect: on/off-ramp, custody and banking services for issuers and corporates
  • Distribute: offer client access to regulated stablecoins within existing channels
  • Issue: launch a bank-branded stablecoin or tokenized deposit

The Bottom Line

Doing nothing is also a position, and it is the one most likely to be decided by someone else. Start with a capability assessment: liquidity impact, compliance readiness, and which client segments will ask first.
